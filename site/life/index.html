The life syntax

It breaks up an animal's life, by whether each part of it is the animal's own or taken by use. It has 5 element types and 10 elements, and a life described in it is one choice of elements from each type.

An animal holds every part of this syntax in full. An animal free at the time has its own in every row; what it is taken from in a row is a use. From the rights position of Split-Domain Cognition's animal-rights variant: no animal captured, confined, fed, trained, tagged or rewarded, and none made to speak for people.

Instances

  • Split-Domain Cognition's animal-rights variant: the rights position the syntax is read from: whether a use of an animal should exist, never how well the animal is treated inside it

Element types and elements

place holds one

Where the animal is.

its own ground
where it goes by itself, free at the time
confined
captured, or kept where people put it

food holds one

What the animal eats, and who brings it.

found by itself
what it finds and takes on its own
fed
given food by people, which ties it to them

action holds one

What the animal does.

its own
what it does of itself
trained or rewarded
shaped by people towards something they want

body holds one

The animal's body.

unmarked
as it is, carrying nothing people put on it
tagged
marked or fitted so that people can find or identify it

signal holds one

What the animal makes heard or seen.

its own, read for structure
units, sequence and rhythm, never put into human words
translated
turned into a human word, as if the animal were a text to be read

Left out

What an animal means, intends or feels. The syntax names what is the animal's own and what is taken by use, never what its signals mean; they are read for structure, never translated. Things and people hold no part of it: every part is the animal's.

Described in it

Nothing is described in it yet.

The syntax as data: life.json. It is read from Split-Domain Cognition each time this site is built, so the two never differ.
